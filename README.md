# NFL Defensive Reaction-Time Analysis

A collaborative football analytics project examining how defensive reaction time relates to pass-play outcomes using NFL Big Data Bowl tracking data.

## Project Overview

On a passing play, a fraction of a second can change everything. This project explored whether differences in defensive reaction time help explain outcomes such as completions, incompletions, pass breakups, and interceptions.

Our group analyzed NFL player-tracking and play data across weeks 1–18 of the season. The broader project identified defensive reactions during passing plays and examined how reaction time interacted with other factors, including defender positioning, distance to the ball, and ball velocity.

## My Role

My main contributions centered on visualization, scenario analysis, and communicating what the results meant in a football context.

I worked on:

* Visualizing differences in defensive reaction time across players and plays.
* Comparing faster- and slower-reacting defenders.
* Examining the relationship between reaction time and player velocity.
* Developing and visualizing “what-if” scenarios that adjusted reaction time from -0.40 to +0.40 seconds in 0.05-second increments.
* Interpreting how small timing adjustments could affect pass-breakup opportunities and other coverage outcomes.
* Contributing to project presentation materials and the communication of our findings.

## Analytical Approach

The full group project combined:

* NFL player-tracking and supplementary play data.
* Change-point analysis to identify a defender’s initial reaction.
* Variables describing reaction time, distance, velocity, and play context.
* A random forest model to evaluate passing outcomes.
* Visual comparisons and counterfactual reaction-time scenarios.

The group’s random forest model reported approximately **76.5% accuracy**. Distance to the ball and ball velocity were particularly influential, while rarer outcomes such as interceptions and pass breakups were more difficult to predict than completions and incompletions.
